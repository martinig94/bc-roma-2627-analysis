# Goal

The goal is to develop tools that improve the work of **scouting and game preparation** of BC Roma.

The idea is to build a seamless process that saves time and turns a very large amount of data (games of two competitions, box scores,
play-by-play, shot locations, lineups) into valuable insights. The tool acts as a **filter and a sense-maker**, and the work is shared in
three steps:

1. **The data work is automated.** Games and statistics are collected every day, and the tables, figures and comparisons of every
   opponent and every game are produced in the same way each time, with no manual work.
2. **The analyst extracts the insights.** Starting from what the automation brings forward, the analyst checks it, adds context and
   plots, discards what is noise and writes down what is valuable: the tendencies of the opponent, the strengths and weaknesses to
   know about, the key players and lineups.
3. **The staff makes the decisions.** The insights are an input for the head coach and the staff, who decide on practices, game plan
   and preparation. The tool supports these decisions; it does not take them.

## Why data analysis matters in sport

- Numbers give a **common, checkable starting point**: what a team actually does (how it shoots, where from, how it scores, who plays
  together) can be compared across games, opponents and competitions, instead of relying on the memory and impressions of a few games.
- Analytics is most useful when it is **combined with the eye of the coach**. Data points to where to look; video and experience say
  why it happens and what to do about it.
- Doing the repetitive part automatically **frees time** for the work that needs a person, and makes the preparation of every game
  follow the same method, so that games and opponents are comparable week after week.

## How it improves

The idea is to use data and technology to optimise the workflow and improve performance through a **continuous process of feedback and
ideas**: what the staff finds useful, and what is missing, comes back as comments on the pages and shapes the next version of the tools.
